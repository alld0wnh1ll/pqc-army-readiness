The road to Q‑Day

From August 2015, the government has been on a road to preparing for a quantum threat. NSA and CNSS directed programs to stop new investment in the public-key crypto that protects command and control and to prepare for quantum-resistant algorithms. This page records that path for the Army. A cryptographically relevant quantum computer (CRQC) makes it easy to derive a private key from a public key. Harvested ciphertext can then be decrypted. An attacker can also sign new orders, forge credentials, and ship software updates that verify as authentic. Forgery is the operational problem.

03 · RequirementsMaximize to readMinimize
Do this before you cut over

Map the network first

Do not wait to buy PQC. Wait only long enough to know your network. If you skip inventory and rush the cutover, you pay twice. Time spent now on inventory, latency, whether systems still talk to each other, and how Soldiers actually use the gear is cheap. Army programs already learned this on WIN-T and JRSS. Write the hard requirements first. Then field PQC.

Spend early money on requirements. Do not rush the cutover. Finish this list first:

  • Find every place RSA and ECC still live
  • Map apps and old networks that depend on that crypto
  • Set latency and throughput limits for bigger keys and slower handshakes
  • Include ICS, OT, and small devices
  • Account for tactical conditions and what operators can handle
  • Get vendor support and a path to swap algorithms later

Skip this and the Army fields gear it has to rework, then does the job again under a tighter clock.

Do the requirements work now. The later bill stays smaller.

Time runs left to right. The teal path does the inventory and requirements work early, so later spend stays modest. The red path waits. Almost nothing happens now, then the cutover takes far more people and money. These curves show the idea. They are not measured data.

Lessons · WIN-T Inc 2

Field gear that formations can use

WIN-T put mobile tactical networking in the field at a scale no one had done before. Testing showed early nodes asked more of formations than operations allow. Army leadership paused, moved about $2.3B, and redesigned the approach. For PQC, write C2, mobility, and Soldier load as hard requirements from day one. Field crypto that units can operate.

Lessons · JRSS

The hard problems show up when you move at scale

JRSS set out to put enterprise security on one modern stack. The stack worked. The hard part showed up during the move: old apps, old networks, ICS and OT paths, and latency. DISA closed those gaps as it went. PQC will hit the same edges because keys get bigger and handshakes get heavier. Find those paths now.

Army PQC work applies these lessons now. Discovery and validation tools find crypto on the network. New vendor solutions enter ATLAS before a Component considers them.

The network keeps getting denser: more function in less gear, more apps on shared paths, less slack. A rushed PQC cutover repeats two failures: gear formations cannot use, and late-found apps, legacy links, ICS, and latency. Inventory shows those paths early.

04 · ATLASMaximize to readMinimize
DoW intake and tracking for PQC solutions

What ATLAS is

ATLAS means Acquisition, Testing, Licensing, and Security. It is the Department of War intake and tracking process for post-quantum cryptography solutions and later work. Industry uses it as the front door for new solutions that use cryptography that is not quantum-vulnerable, before a Component considers the product. Components use it to track what is approved, from pre-testing through deployment, and to see certification status for national security systems plus test results from other Components.

How it fits

Front door before Component review

Industry enters here first. ATLAS records intake and tracks status. A Component still decides whether to test, buy, or field the product. ATLAS does not replace NIST standards, CNSA 2.0, or a program's own test.

Who submits

The government sponsor files the form

The government sponsor submits this questionnaire. The form collects common data points. The vendor receives the items in Question 13 and emails the accompanying documents. Incomplete packets close 7 days after the sponsor starts the submission.

What it tracks

Status and results across Components

ATLAS tracks approved solutions and their stage. It shows NSS certification status and performance-test outcomes from other Components. It is a reference map of cryptographic readiness and usability. It is not a blanket Department-wide approval.
